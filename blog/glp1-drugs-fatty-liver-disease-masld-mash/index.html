Can GLP-1 Drugs Treat Fatty Liver Disease (MASLD and MASH)?

Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Yes, for some people. In August 2025 the FDA approved semaglutide (Wegovy) to treat MASH with moderate to advanced liver scarring in adults without cirrhosis. In the ESSENCE trial, 63% of people on semaglutide had their liver inflammation resolve, compared with 34% on placebo. Other GLP-1-based drugs, such as tirzepatide, show strong early results but are not yet approved for liver disease.

What are MASLD and MASH?

MASLD stands for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease. It is the new name for nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD). The National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK) defines it as excess fat in the liver that heavy alcohol use does not explain.

MASH (formerly NASH) is the more serious form. In MASH, the liver has fat plus inflammation and cell damage. Over time, this damage can cause scarring (fibrosis) and then cirrhosis.

Fatty liver disease is common. NIDDK estimates that about 24% of U.S. adults have NAFLD and about 1.5% to 6.5% have NASH. It is closely tied to excess weight and diabetes:

  • Up to 75% of people with overweight have fatty liver disease.
  • More than 90% of people with severe obesity have it.
  • One-third to two-thirds of people with type 2 diabetes have it.

Doctors grade liver scarring from F0 (none) to F4 (cirrhosis). Stages F2 and F3 are moderate and advanced fibrosis. This is the group the current drug approvals target.

Is any GLP-1 drug FDA approved for MASH?

Yes. Semaglutide 2.4 mg injection (Wegovy) is the only GLP-1 drug with a MASH indication. The FDA announced the approval in August 2025 under its accelerated approval pathway.

The Wegovy prescribing information lists the indication as noncirrhotic MASH with moderate to advanced liver fibrosis (stages F2 to F3) in adults. It is used with a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity. The labeled maintenance dose for MASH is 2.4 mg once weekly. The dose can drop to 1.7 mg if a person cannot tolerate 2.4 mg.

A few details matter here:

  • Accelerated approval. The FDA based the decision on liver biopsy improvements, not on proven reductions in cirrhosis, transplant, or death. The label says continued approval may depend on a confirmatory trial.
  • Injection only. The label lists the MASH indication for Wegovy injection. Wegovy tablets carry weight and heart indications, not MASH.
  • No cirrhosis. People with cirrhosis (F4) are outside the approved use.

Semaglutide is the second drug approved for this condition. The first was resmetirom (Rezdiffra), a non-GLP-1 pill that the FDA approved on March 14, 2024.

What did the ESSENCE trial show?

ESSENCE is a phase 3 trial that enrolled 1,197 adults with biopsy-confirmed MASH and F2 or F3 fibrosis. Participants received semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo once weekly. The trial is planned to run 240 weeks.

The part 1 results, published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2025, cover the first 800 patients at week 72:

  • Steatohepatitis resolved without worse fibrosis in 62.9% on semaglutide versus 34.3% on placebo.
  • Fibrosis improved without worse steatohepatitis in 36.8% versus 22.4%.
  • Both outcomes together occurred in 32.7% versus 16.1%.
  • Average weight change was -10.5% with semaglutide and -2.0% with placebo.

Stomach and bowel side effects were more common with semaglutide. Part 2 of the trial will look at hard outcomes such as cirrhosis, liver transplant, and death.

How do other GLP-1-based drugs compare?

Several drugs in the class have liver biopsy data. None besides Wegovy is approved for MASH, and cross-trial comparisons are unreliable because the trials differ in size, length, and patients.

  • Tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound). In the phase 2 SYNERGY-NASH trial, 190 adults with F2 or F3 fibrosis took tirzepatide or placebo for 52 weeks. MASH resolved in 44%, 56%, and 62% on the 5, 10, and 15 mg doses, versus 10% on placebo. The current Zepbound label does not include a MASH indication. Our sibling site covers this drug in detail in Mounjaro for fatty liver and MASH.
  • Survodutide. This experimental glucagon and GLP-1 dual agonist was tested in a 48-week phase 2 trial of 293 adults with F1 to F3 fibrosis. MASH improved without worse fibrosis in 43% to 62% across doses, versus 14% on placebo. Phase 3 trials are needed.
  • Liraglutide (Victoza, Saxenda). The small LEAN trial in 2016 was an early signal. NASH resolved in 9 of 23 patients (39%) on liraglutide versus 2 of 22 (9%) on placebo.

How do GLP-1 drugs help the liver?

Weight loss is the main driver. NIDDK notes that losing at least 3% to 5% of body weight can reduce liver fat. Losing up to 7% to 10% may be needed to reduce inflammation and fibrosis. GLP-1 drugs help many people reach these targets.

The drugs also improve blood sugar and insulin resistance, which drive fat buildup in the liver. Researchers are still studying whether there are direct liver effects beyond weight loss. Dual agonists add GIP or glucagon activity, and drug makers hope this gives extra liver benefit. That idea still needs proof in phase 3 trials.

Who is a candidate, and what are the limits?

Under the label, the candidate is an adult with MASH and F2 or F3 fibrosis but no cirrhosis. Doctors usually confirm the stage with blood tests, imaging such as elastography, or a biopsy. People with simple fatty liver (no inflammation or scarring) do not fall under the MASH indication. Some may still qualify for a GLP-1 drug for weight or diabetes.

Keep these limits in mind:

  • The benefit on cirrhosis, liver failure, and survival is not yet proven.
  • The label notes limited information in MASH patients with a BMI under 25.
  • Side effects are the same as in other uses: mostly nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, and constipation. See our GLP-1 side effects guide.
  • Rapid weight loss and poor nutrition can make liver disease worse, according to NIDDK.

Insurance coverage for the MASH indication varies by plan. A liver specialist can help decide between semaglutide, resmetirom, or lifestyle treatment alone.

The bottom line

GLP-1 drugs can treat MASH, the inflammatory form of fatty liver disease. Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4 mg injection) is FDA approved for adults with MASH and F2 to F3 fibrosis without cirrhosis, based on the ESSENCE trial. Tirzepatide and survodutide show promising biopsy results but are not yet approved for this use. Long-term proof that these drugs prevent cirrhosis and liver failure is still pending.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Ozempic treat fatty liver disease?

Ozempic contains semaglutide, but its label covers type 2 diabetes and related uses, not MASH. The MASH indication belongs to Wegovy, which is semaglutide at a 2.4 mg weekly dose. A doctor may still consider liver health when choosing a diabetes drug.

Can GLP-1 drugs reverse liver scarring?

In the ESSENCE trial, fibrosis improved by at least one stage in 36.8% of people on semaglutide versus 22.4% on placebo. So scarring improved in some people, but not in most. Whether this prevents cirrhosis long term is still under study.

Is Wegovy approved for people with cirrhosis from MASH?

No. The Wegovy label covers noncirrhotic MASH with stage F2 to F3 fibrosis in adults. People with cirrhosis (F4) are outside the approved use, and trials in that group are ongoing.

How long does it take to see liver improvement on semaglutide?

The ESSENCE trial measured liver biopsy results at 72 weeks, which is about 17 months. Liver fat may drop sooner as weight falls, but the approved claims rest on the 72-week biopsy data.

What is the difference between Wegovy and Rezdiffra for MASH?

Both are FDA approved for adults with noncirrhotic MASH and moderate to advanced fibrosis. Wegovy is a weekly GLP-1 injection that also causes weight loss. Rezdiffra (resmetirom) is a daily pill that acts on a thyroid hormone receptor in the liver.
