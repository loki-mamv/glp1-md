Do GLP-1 Drugs Cause Loose Skin After Weight Loss?

October 5, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Not directly. Neither the Wegovy label nor the Zepbound label lists loose or sagging skin as a side effect. The cause is the amount of weight lost. In the main trials, people lost about 15% to 21% of their body weight. Skin does not always shrink to fit a smaller body. No trial has measured skin laxity on these drugs, so nobody can give you an exact risk.

Do the FDA labels list loose skin as a side effect?

No. We searched the full text of both weight-loss labels for this article. The Wegovy (semaglutide) prescribing information and the Zepbound (tirzepatide) prescribing information do not contain the words "laxity," "sagging," or "loose skin."

The labels do list other skin and hair effects:

  • Hair loss. Both labels list it. Both state that it was "associated with weight reduction." We cover that in our post on GLP-1 hair loss.
  • Dysesthesia. This is an altered skin sensation, such as burning or sensitive skin. It is a nerve sensation. It is not a change in skin firmness.
  • Injection site reactions and skin rashes from hypersensitivity.

A label lists what the trials recorded as adverse reactions. The trials did not measure skin firmness. So the labels are silent on loose skin. That silence does not prove the drugs have no effect on skin. It means nobody collected the data.

Why does skin loosen after major weight loss?

Skin stretches to cover a larger body. When the fat under it shrinks, the skin can stay stretched. The more weight a person loses, the more surface is left over.

The weight loss on these drugs is large. In STEP 1, adults on semaglutide 2.4 mg lost a mean of 14.9% of body weight in 68 weeks. That was 15.3 kg (about 34 pounds). In SURMOUNT-1, adults on tirzepatide lost a mean of 15.0% to 20.9% in 72 weeks, by dose. On the 15 mg dose, 57% of people lost 20% or more.

The loss under the skin is mostly fat. In the SURMOUNT-1 body-composition substudy, fat mass fell 33.9% and lean mass fell 10.9% on tirzepatide. About 75% of the weight lost was fat. The placebo group lost weight in the same 75 to 25 proportion. See our post on GLP-1 muscle loss for the details.

Insurance-claims data fit this picture. A study of a US claims database found higher rates of body contouring surgery among GLP-1 users than non-users. The correlations were weak (r = 0.21 to 0.28), and the study cannot prove cause.

What happens inside the skin?

The evidence here comes from people who had bariatric surgery or lost weight by diet. It does not come from GLP-1 users. The studies are small, and they do not fully agree.

  • A study of 40 patients compared abdominal skin after massive weight loss with skin from people with severe obesity. Total collagen was the same. But the weight-loss group had fewer thick, organized collagen fibers and more thin, loosely arranged fibers. Elastic fiber density was higher.
  • A biomechanical study tested skin from 35 massive weight loss patients. Elastin fibers were decreased. But the skin was not weaker in a burst test. The authors called their own results a contradiction.
  • A 2024 study of 77 patients compared surgical with non-surgical massive weight loss. Collagen content was similar in both groups. Abdominal skin had more elastic fiber content in the non-surgical group.

The plain reading: massive weight loss changes the structure of the skin's support fibers. The exact change is not settled. No study has taken skin biopsies from GLP-1 users, so the application to these drugs is an inference.

Is "Ozempic face" the same thing?

It is related. "Ozempic face" is a media term for a hollow, older-looking face after weight loss. The measured cause is fat loss in the face.

One imaging study measured it. Researchers compared head scans from before and after GLP-1 treatment in 20 patients. Median midface volume fell 9%. The loss was mainly in the superficial fat pads. The authors estimated a 7% loss of midface volume for each 10 kg of weight lost.

That study is small and retrospective. It measured fat volume. It did not measure skin quality. A 2025 systematic review of 23 plastic surgery articles described the facial changes as resembling advanced aging. The authors noted that study bias limits that literature. Our sibling site has a full post on Ozempic face.

Can you prevent loose skin on a GLP-1 drug?

No proven method exists. We found no trial that tested a way to prevent loose skin during GLP-1 treatment.

Here is what we can and cannot say:

  • Creams and collagen supplements. None of the sources we loaded showed that these tighten skin after weight loss. We make no claim that they work.
  • Fillers and energy-based devices. A 2025 commentary in a cosmetic dermatology journal lists these as options. That is expert opinion. The authors themselves point to gaps in empirical data, and they report honoraria from aesthetic product companies.
  • Slower weight loss. Clinicians often say rapid loss makes laxity worse. We found no trial that compared fast with slow loss for skin outcomes. Do not change your dose for this reason. Dose changes belong to your prescriber.
  • Strength training and protein. These have evidence for protecting muscle. Muscle fills some of the space under the skin. No study shows that they tighten the skin itself.

Age and the total amount of weight lost are widely cited risk factors. In the studies above, they were not tested in GLP-1 users.

When do surgeons consider body contouring?

Surgery to remove excess skin is the established treatment. The timing rule is weight stability. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons lists "adults whose weight loss has stabilized" first among good candidates. It also lists non-smokers and people without conditions that impair healing. It does not give a number of months.

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis looked at timing for semaglutide users. It pooled 12 studies with 13,947 patients. Weight loss reached a plateau at about 53 weeks, at about 16% weight loss. The authors concluded that weight stabilizes after 8 to 12 months of semaglutide. At that point, they wrote, surgery "may be appropriate." They noted that weight stabilizes 12 to 18 months after bariatric surgery.

The same review gave a caution. Two studies followed people after they stopped the drug. Participants regained 62.7% and 74.3% of the weight they had lost. Weight change after surgery can distort the result. The authors advised surgeons to be careful until more research is done.

Tell your surgeon and your anesthesia team that you take a GLP-1 drug. Our post on GLP-1 drugs before surgery explains why.

The bottom line

GLP-1 drugs do not appear on any FDA label as a cause of loose skin. The weight loss is the cause, and it is large: about 15% with semaglutide and up to 21% with tirzepatide. Small biopsy studies show that massive weight loss changes skin collagen and elastic fibers. One small imaging study shows facial fat loss in GLP-1 users. No trial has measured skin laxity on these drugs, and no cream, supplement, or device has trial evidence in this group. Surgery is the proven fix, and surgeons want stable weight first. Talk with your prescriber before you change anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is loose skin listed as a side effect of Wegovy or Zepbound?

No. The current Wegovy and Zepbound prescribing information does not contain the terms laxity, sagging, or loose skin. Both labels list hair loss and state that it was associated with weight reduction.

Will loose skin tighten by itself after GLP-1 weight loss?

No study has followed skin recovery in GLP-1 users, so the answer is unknown. Small biopsy studies after massive weight loss show changes in collagen and elastic fibers. Those studies did not track whether the skin recovered over time.

Does collagen powder or firming cream fix loose skin after weight loss?

We found no primary source that shows a collagen supplement or a cream tightens skin after GLP-1 weight loss. A 2025 commentary lists fillers and energy-based devices as options, but it is expert opinion and it notes gaps in the data.

How long should weight be stable before skin removal surgery?

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons says good candidates are adults whose weight loss has stabilized, and gives no fixed number of months. A 2025 meta-analysis found that weight on semaglutide stabilizes after about 8 to 12 months. Your surgeon sets the timing for your case.

Does tirzepatide cause more loose skin than semaglutide?

No study has compared skin outcomes between the two drugs. Tirzepatide produced more weight loss in its main trial: up to 20.9% in SURMOUNT-1, against 14.9% for semaglutide in STEP 1. More weight loss leaves more excess skin, so a higher risk is a reasonable inference and not a measured fact.
