Can Teenagers Take GLP-1 Drugs for Weight Loss, and Which Ones Are FDA Approved?

The short answer: Yes, some teenagers can. The FDA approves two GLP-1 drugs for weight management in children aged 12 and older with obesity: Wegovy injection (semaglutide) and Saxenda (liraglutide). Zepbound and Ozempic are not approved for anyone under 18. The American Academy of Pediatrics says doctors should offer these medicines to teens 12 and older with obesity, alongside lifestyle treatment.

Can teenagers legally be prescribed GLP-1 drugs for weight loss?

Yes, within limits set by each drug's FDA label. A label lists the ages and conditions the FDA has reviewed. For weight management, the youngest labeled age for any GLP-1 drug is 12 years.

The labels use pediatric definitions of obesity, not adult BMI cut-offs. In children and teens, obesity means a BMI at or above the 95th percentile for age and sex. Overweight means the 85th to below the 95th percentile, as the American Academy of Pediatrics defines it.

This matters because the teen indications cover obesity only. The adult labels also cover people with overweight plus a weight-related condition. The teen labels do not.

Doctors can prescribe a drug "off label" outside these limits. But insurance coverage, safety data, and dosing guidance are all built around the labeled uses.

Which GLP-1 drugs are FDA approved for teens with obesity?

As of October 2026, two products carry a pediatric weight management indication:

  • Wegovy injection (semaglutide), once weekly. The current label covers "adults and pediatric patients aged 12 years and older with obesity," used with a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity. For teens, the labeled maintenance dose is 2.4 mg weekly (recommended) or 1.7 mg weekly.
  • Saxenda (liraglutide), once daily. The Saxenda label covers patients aged 12 and older with obesity and a body weight above 60 kg (about 132 pounds). It states that safety and effectiveness have not been established under age 12.

Two details are easy to miss. First, Wegovy tablets (oral semaglutide for weight) are labeled for adults only. The teen indication applies to the injection. Second, the Saxenda label says its safety and effectiveness have not been established in pediatric patients with type 2 diabetes.

What about Zepbound, Ozempic, and the diabetes drugs?

Zepbound (tirzepatide) is approved only for adults. Its label states that safety and effectiveness "have not been established in pediatric patients." Ozempic (semaglutide) says the same in its label. Ozempic is a type 2 diabetes drug for adults, not a weight loss drug for teens.

Several GLP-1-based drugs are approved for children with type 2 diabetes, starting at age 10. These approvals are for blood sugar control, not weight management:

  • Victoza (liraglutide): adults and pediatric patients aged 10 and older with type 2 diabetes.
  • Trulicity (dulaglutide): adults and pediatric patients 10 years of age and older with type 2 diabetes.
  • Mounjaro (tirzepatide): adults and pediatric patients 10 years of age and older with type 2 diabetes. The label cites a 30-week placebo-controlled trial in 99 pediatric patients.

So tirzepatide is approved for some children, but only as Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes. Teens may lose weight on these drugs, but weight loss is not the labeled purpose. For more on the tirzepatide brand family, see mounjaro.md.

How much weight did teens lose in the trials?

Each approval rests on a placebo-controlled trial in adolescents aged 12 to under 18. Both trials started with a 12-week lifestyle run-in period, and all participants kept lifestyle counseling.

STEP TEENS (semaglutide 2.4 mg). This 68-week trial enrolled 201 adolescents. It was published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2022. Key results:

  • BMI fell 16.1% with semaglutide and rose 0.6% with placebo.
  • 73% of teens on semaglutide lost at least 5% of body weight, compared with 18% on placebo.
  • The Wegovy label reports body weight fell 14.7% on semaglutide and rose 2.7% on placebo.
  • Waist size, HbA1c, most lipids, and the liver enzyme ALT improved more than with placebo.

Liraglutide adolescent trial (Saxenda 3.0 mg). This 56-week trial enrolled 251 adolescents and was published in 2020. The effect was smaller:

  • Body weight fell 2.65% on liraglutide and rose 2.37% on placebo, a difference of about 5 percentage points.
  • 43.3% on liraglutide reached at least a 5% BMI reduction, compared with 18.7% on placebo.
  • After the drug stopped, BMI scores rose more in the liraglutide group than in the placebo group.

That last point mirrors adult data. Weight tends to return when treatment ends. See what happens when you stop taking GLP-1 drugs.

What do pediatricians recommend?

The American Academy of Pediatrics published its first full clinical practice guideline on child and adolescent obesity in Pediatrics in February 2023. On medication, the AAP summary says physicians should offer adolescents ages 12 and older with obesity weight loss pharmacotherapy, "according to medication indications, risks, and benefits, as an adjunct to health behavior and lifestyle treatment."

The guideline places drugs inside a broader plan, not in place of one:

  • Intensive health behavior and lifestyle treatment is the most effective known behavioral treatment. The best programs give 26 or more hours of family-based care over 3 to 12 months.
  • Teens 13 and older with severe obesity (BMI at least 120% of the 95th percentile) should be evaluated for metabolic and bariatric surgery.

The guideline does not set an age below 12 for drug treatment, which matches the current FDA labels.

What side effects and monitoring matter for teens?

Side effects in teens look much like those in adults. Stomach and gut symptoms lead the list. In the Wegovy teen trial, the label reports nausea in 42% on semaglutide versus 18% on placebo, and vomiting in 36% versus 10%.

The labels and trials flag several points specific to younger patients:

  • Gallbladder problems. The Wegovy label says teens had higher rates of gallstones, gallbladder inflammation, low blood pressure, rash, and hives than adults. Gallstones occurred in 3.8% on semaglutide and 0% on placebo.
  • Low blood sugar. The Saxenda label reports more confirmed low blood sugar episodes in teens on liraglutide. The Wegovy label advises checking blood glucose before and during treatment in teens with type 2 diabetes.
  • Heart rate and pancreatitis. In the liraglutide teen trial, resting heart rate rose 3 to 7 beats per minute. One treated patient had pancreatitis.
  • Stopping due to side effects. 10.4% of teens on liraglutide stopped treatment because of an adverse reaction, compared with none on placebo.

All GLP-1 labels carry a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents. These drugs are not for anyone with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2. The bottom line

Teenagers 12 and older with obesity can be prescribed Wegovy injection or Saxenda, the only GLP-1 drugs the FDA approves for pediatric weight management. Zepbound, Ozempic, and Wegovy tablets are for adults only. Victoza, Trulicity, and Mounjaro are approved from age 10, but for type 2 diabetes. In trials, semaglutide cut teen BMI by about 16%, far more than liraglutide. Pediatric guidelines support medicine as one part of a family-based lifestyle plan, with close follow-up.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a GLP-1 drug approved for children under 12 with obesity?

No. As of October 2026, no GLP-1 drug carries an FDA weight management indication below age 12. The Saxenda label states that safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 12. Some GLP-1 drugs are approved from age 10, but only for type 2 diabetes.

Can a teen with overweight but not obesity get Wegovy?

Not under the label. The teen indication for Wegovy injection covers obesity, defined as a BMI at or above the 95th percentile for age and sex. The overweight-plus-comorbidity indication applies to adults only.

Is Wegovy or Saxenda more effective for adolescents?

In separate trials, semaglutide produced much larger results. Teens on Wegovy injection had a 16.1% drop in BMI over 68 weeks, while teens on Saxenda had a 4.29% drop over 56 weeks. The two drugs were not compared head to head in the same trial.

Does a teen need to keep taking a GLP-1 drug to keep the weight off?

The evidence suggests weight tends to return after stopping. In the liraglutide adolescent trial, BMI scores rose more in the drug group than in the placebo group after treatment ended. Families should talk with their doctor about long-term plans before starting.

Can a teenager take Mounjaro for weight loss?

Mounjaro is FDA approved for children 10 and older with type 2 diabetes, to control blood sugar. It is not approved for weight management in anyone. Zepbound, the tirzepatide brand for weight, is approved for adults only.
