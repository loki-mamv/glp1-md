What Causes Sulfur Burps on GLP-1 Drugs, and How Do You Stop Them?

The short answer: Burping (the label term is eructation) is a listed side effect of semaglutide and tirzepatide. In the Wegovy trials it affected 7% of adults, against less than 1% on placebo. The drugs slow stomach emptying, which is the likely cause. No study has shown why the burps smell of sulfur in GLP-1 users, so that part is a hypothesis. Smaller meals, less swallowed air, and time are the measures with source support.

How common is burping on GLP-1 drugs?

The FDA labels count burps as "eructation". They do not separate ordinary burps from sulfur burps. The rates below come from the adverse reaction sections of each label.

  • Ozempic (semaglutide, type 2 diabetes): 0% on placebo, 2.7% on 0.5 mg, and 1.1% on 1 mg, per the Ozempic label.
  • Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4 mg, weight loss): 7% of adults, against less than 1% on placebo. In patients aged 12 and older, the rate was 4% against 0% on placebo, per the Wegovy label.
  • Mounjaro (tirzepatide, type 2 diabetes): 0.4% on placebo, 3.0% on 5 mg, 2.5% on 10 mg, and 3.3% on 15 mg, per the Mounjaro label.
  • Zepbound (tirzepatide, weight loss): 1% on placebo, 4% on 5 mg, 5% on 10 mg, and 5% on 15 mg, per the Zepbound label.

Two patterns show in these numbers. Burping is much more common on the drug than on placebo in every label. The rates are also higher in the weight loss trials than in the diabetes trials of the same drug. The numbers come from different trials, so do not use them to rank the drugs.

Some burping is normal. The National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK) says people typically belch up to 30 times a day. The trial numbers count people who reported burping as a problem.

Why do GLP-1 drugs cause more burping?

The best supported cause is slower stomach emptying. All four labels state that the drug delays gastric emptying. Food stays in the stomach longer, and gas has more time to come back up.

One small trial measured the size of this effect. Researchers gave 20 women with obesity and polycystic ovary syndrome semaglutide 1.0 mg or placebo for 12 weeks. Four hours after a test meal, the semaglutide group still held 37% of the meal in the stomach. The placebo group held none. Half the meal took 171 minutes to empty on semaglutide and 118 minutes on placebo, according to the study in Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism.

The effect can fade. The Mounjaro label says the delay is largest after the first dose and gets smaller over time. A Mayo Clinic review of GLP-1 drugs and stomach function also reports that the effect on stomach emptying drops with long-acting drugs. The Wegovy label says burping and related stomach reactions were reported most often during dose escalation.

The labels do not say that slow emptying causes the burping. It is the most likely explanation, but the trials did not test it. For more on how these drugs act on the gut, see how GLP-1 drugs work.

What makes the burps smell like sulfur?

The smell of rotten eggs is the smell of hydrogen sulfide gas. Gut bacteria make this gas. In a study of 16 healthy adults published in Gut, hydrogen sulfide was the main sulfur gas in intestinal gas. The strength of the odor tracked the hydrogen sulfide level. NIDDK also says the odor of passed gas may be due to sulfur.

That research measured gas passed from the bowel. It did not measure burps, and it did not study people on GLP-1 drugs. We found no primary study that measured hydrogen sulfide in the burps of people who take semaglutide or tirzepatide.

The common explanation is that food sits longer in the gut, bacteria act on it, and sulfur gas comes up. This is a reasonable hypothesis. It is not proven. No label uses the words "sulfur burps", and no trial counted them apart from ordinary burps.

How do you stop sulfur burps on a GLP-1 drug?

No clinical trial has tested a treatment for burping caused by GLP-1 drugs. The labels give no diet advice for it. The measures below come from NIDDK guidance on gas in general. They are low risk, but they are not proven for this use.

  • Eat smaller meals more often. NIDDK diet guidance lists smaller, more frequent meals in place of large meals.
  • Eat slowly and sit down to eat. People swallow more air when they eat or drink too fast.
  • Skip fizzy drinks and straws. NIDDK treatment guidance says swallowing less air may help, mainly for people who belch a lot.
  • Avoid chewing gum and hard candy. Both increase swallowed air.
  • Look at high-fat foods. NIDDK says high-fat foods can increase bloating in some people.
  • Keep a food and symptom diary. NIDDK says a diary helps a doctor or dietitian find the foods that set off your symptoms.

You will see advice online to cut eggs, red meat, garlic, or broccoli because they contain sulfur. No primary source we read tested this for burps in GLP-1 users.

NIDDK says doctors may recommend over-the-counter medicines or supplements for gas symptoms. It names no product for sulfur burps, and we found no trial of any product for this problem. NIDDK also says to talk with your doctor before you use supplements or probiotics.

Dose matters too. If the burps started after a dose increase, tell your prescriber. Do not change the dose or the schedule yourself. If you take a brand-name product, the pages at ozempic.md and mounjaro.md cover each drug in more detail.

When are burps a sign of something serious?

Burping alone is rarely dangerous. Burping with other symptoms can be. The labels carry several warnings that apply here.

  • Pancreatitis. The Wegovy and Zepbound labels describe severe abdominal pain that can spread to the back, with or without nausea or vomiting. The labels say to stop the drug and contact a doctor promptly.
  • Severe stomach and bowel reactions. Severe reactions occurred in 4.1% of adults on Wegovy injection and 0.9% on placebo. The labels say to contact your healthcare provider for severe or persistent symptoms.
  • Bowel blockage. Ileus and intestinal obstruction appear in the postmarketing reports for all four drugs. The labels cannot give a rate for these reports.
  • Dehydration. Vomiting or diarrhea that does not stop can lead to kidney injury. The labels tell patients to report persistent nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea.
  • Gallbladder disease. The Wegovy label says gallbladder disease can occur with or without rapid weight loss.

Get medical help the same day for severe belly pain, repeated vomiting, or an inability to keep fluids down. The labels do not recommend these drugs for people with severe gastroparesis. See who should not take GLP-1 drugs for the full list.

NIDDK adds a general rule for gas. Talk with your doctor if the symptoms bother you, change suddenly, or come with belly pain, constipation, diarrhea, or weight loss.

Do sulfur burps go away over time?

The data point to yes for many people, but no trial followed sulfur burps over time. The Wegovy label says stomach and bowel reactions were reported most often during dose escalation. The Mounjaro label says most reports of nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea occurred during dose escalation and decreased over time.

Burping is one part of a wider set of stomach effects. Our complete guide to GLP-1 side effects covers nausea, constipation, and the rest.

The bottom line

Burping is a real, labeled side effect of GLP-1 drugs. Rates in the labels run from 1.1% to 7% on the drug, against 0% to 1% on placebo. Slow stomach emptying is the likely cause. The sulfur smell most likely comes from hydrogen sulfide made by gut bacteria, but no study has confirmed this in GLP-1 users. Smaller meals and less swallowed air are sensible first steps. Severe pain or vomiting that does not stop needs prompt medical care.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are sulfur burps listed on the Ozempic or Wegovy label?

The labels list eructation, which is the medical term for burping. They do not use the words sulfur burps and do not count them apart from ordinary burps. The Ozempic label reports eructation in 2.7% of patients on 0.5 mg and 1.1% on 1 mg, against 0% on placebo. The Wegovy label reports 7% of adults against less than 1% on placebo.

Does tirzepatide cause fewer burps than semaglutide?

The labels cannot answer this. Zepbound reports eructation in 4% to 5% of patients and Wegovy reports 7%, but these numbers come from different trials with different patients. No head-to-head trial has compared burping rates between the two drugs.

Is there a medicine that stops sulfur burps from GLP-1 drugs?

No clinical trial has tested any medicine or supplement for burping caused by GLP-1 drugs. NIDDK says doctors may recommend over-the-counter medicines for gas symptoms in general, but it names no product for sulfur burps. Ask your prescriber or pharmacist before you add a product.

Should I stop my GLP-1 drug because of sulfur burps?

Do not stop or change your dose without your prescriber. Burping alone is a known and usually mild side effect. The labels say to stop the drug and contact a doctor promptly if you have severe abdominal pain that could be pancreatitis.

Why do the burps smell like rotten eggs?

The rotten egg smell is hydrogen sulfide, a sulfur gas that gut bacteria make. A study in the journal Gut found it was the main sulfur gas in intestinal gas and that it tracked with odor. No study has measured it in the burps of GLP-1 users, so the link is a hypothesis.
