Do GLP-1 Drugs Make Birth Control Pills and Other Oral Medicines Less Effective?

October 9, 2026 7 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: For most GLP-1 drugs, studies found no meaningful drop in birth control pill absorption. Tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) is the exception with data: one dose cut the pill's peak hormone levels by 55% to 66%. Its label, and the label for the newer pill Foundayo (orforglipron), tell patients on oral contraceptives to switch to a non-oral method or add a barrier method for a set period. Every label in the class also advises caution with other oral medicines, because these drugs slow stomach emptying.

Why would a GLP-1 drug change how a pill is absorbed?

GLP-1 drugs slow the rate at which the stomach empties. The labels say this delay "has the potential to impact the absorption of concomitantly administered oral medications." The same effect drives many of the stomach complaints covered in our GLP-1 side effects guide.

Drug makers test the delay with acetaminophen as a marker. In the tirzepatide test, the peak blood level fell and arrived later, but the total amount absorbed did not change.

The delay is not constant. The Mounjaro label says the effect on stomach emptying was greatest after a single 5 mg dose and diminished after later doses. After that first dose, the acetaminophen peak fell by 50% and came 1 hour later. By week 4 there was no meaningful effect, and total acetaminophen exposure was not changed. The Trulicity label describes the same pattern for dulaglutide: the delay "is largest after the first dose and diminishes with subsequent doses."

Which GLP-1 labels give a birth control instruction?

Two active ingredients carry a specific contraception instruction in the drug interactions section. The rest do not.

  • Mounjaro and Zepbound (tirzepatide): Both labels say to "advise patients using oral hormonal contraceptives to switch to a non-oral contraceptive method or add a barrier method of contraception for 4 weeks after initiation and for 4 weeks after each dose escalation." They add that hormonal contraceptives that are not taken by mouth "should not be affected" (Mounjaro label, Zepbound label).
  • Foundayo (orforglipron): The label for this once-daily weight-loss pill gives the same instruction with a window of 30 days after starting and 30 days after each dose escalation. It also states that the effect on oral contraceptive absorption "has not been evaluated in a clinical trial" (Foundayo label).
  • Ozempic and Wegovy (semaglutide): No contraception instruction. The Ozempic label says semaglutide "did not affect the absorption of orally administered medications to any clinically relevant degree," and still advises caution.
  • Rybelsus and Ozempic tablets (oral semaglutide): No contraception instruction. The label lists ethinyl estradiol and levonorgestrel among drugs with no clinically significant change.
  • Victoza and Saxenda (liraglutide): No contraception instruction (Victoza label, Saxenda label).
  • Trulicity (dulaglutide): No contraception instruction in the drug interactions section.

The tirzepatide labels explain the reason in one line: the drug "may reduce the efficacy of oral hormonal contraceptives due to delayed gastric emptying." Our sister site has a full walk-through in Mounjaro and birth control.

What did the birth control pill studies find?

The pharmacokinetic studies measured hormone levels in blood. None of them measured pregnancy rates.

  • Tirzepatide: A combined pill (0.035 mg ethinyl estradiol and 0.25 mg norgestimate) was given with a single 5 mg dose. Peak levels of ethinyl estradiol, norgestimate and norelgestromin fell by 59%, 66% and 55%. Total exposure fell by 20%, 21% and 23%. The peak came 2.5 to 4.5 hours later (Mounjaro label).
  • Injectable semaglutide: In 43 postmenopausal women with type 2 diabetes, semaglutide 1.0 mg at steady state did not reduce exposure to ethinylestradiol or levonorgestrel. Levonorgestrel exposure was 20% higher (Journal of Clinical Pharmacology, 2015).
  • Oral semaglutide: In 25 healthy postmenopausal women, exposure to both hormones was not affected. The estimated ratio was 1.06 for each (Clinical Pharmacokinetics, 2021).
  • Liraglutide: Peak ethinylestradiol and levonorgestrel fell by 12% and 13%, and the peak came 1.5 hours later. Total ethinylestradiol exposure did not change (Victoza label).

A 2024 review in the Journal of the American Pharmacists Association covered 6 trials. Only the tirzepatide trial showed a significant fall in contraceptive hormone levels. The 5 trials of GLP-1 receptor agonists did not.

Two limits apply. First, a lower peak is not the same as a failed pill. A 2026 review in Maturitas notes that a fall in peak concentration "cannot be equated with reduced efficacy." Second, the semaglutide trials used the 1 mg injection and postmenopausal volunteers, not the 2.4 mg weight-loss dose.

Which other oral medicines do the labels name?

The labels single out drugs with a narrow therapeutic index. These are medicines where a small change in blood level matters.

  • Warfarin: The tirzepatide labels say to monitor patients on oral drugs "dependent on threshold concentrations for efficacy and those with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., warfarin)." The Trulicity label says to "monitor drug levels of oral medications with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., warfarin)."
  • Levothyroxine: In a study with semaglutide tablets, levothyroxine exposure rose by 33% after a single 600 mcg dose. The oral semaglutide label and the Wegovy label both cite this figure. Both say to consider increased clinical or laboratory monitoring for narrow therapeutic index drugs.
  • Simvastatin: The Foundayo label says not to exceed simvastatin 20 mg once daily, because orforglipron doubled exposure to the active form of simvastatin. This is a metabolism effect, not a stomach emptying effect.
  • Furosemide and rosuvastatin: Exposure rose by 28% and 41% with oral semaglutide. The study authors did not expect this to be clinically relevant (Clinical Pharmacokinetics, 2021).

The labels for injectable semaglutide and liraglutide report no clinically relevant change for the drugs they tested. A 2025 review in Drug Design, Development and Therapy reached the same overall view. It found most stomach emptying interactions clinically insignificant, with two exceptions: oral contraceptives with tirzepatide, and levothyroxine with oral semaglutide.

Do GLP-1 pills need to be taken apart from other medicines?

For semaglutide tablets, the label sets a timing rule. The Rybelsus and Ozempic tablets label and the Wegovy tablet label say to take the tablet on an empty stomach with up to 4 ounces of water. Both (oral semaglutide label, Wegovy label) then say to "wait at least 30 minutes before eating food, drinking beverages or taking other oral medications."

That rule protects the absorption of semaglutide itself. It is a different issue from the effect of slow stomach emptying on other pills. For how the pill forms compare with the shots, see oral GLP-1 pills vs injections.

The drug interactions sections of the injectable labels state no separation time for other oral medicines. They advise caution or monitoring instead.

What should you ask your prescriber or pharmacist?

The labels direct their instructions to prescribers. These are the questions they raise for a patient:

  • Which GLP-1 drug am I on, and does its label carry a contraception instruction?
  • Am I starting the drug or increasing the dose? The tirzepatide and orforglipron windows restart after each dose escalation.
  • Do I take warfarin, levothyroxine or another medicine that needs blood tests? Ask whether the test schedule should change.

Do not stop or re-time a prescribed medicine on your own. Pregnancy matters here because these drugs are not approved for use in pregnancy. Our article on GLP-1 drugs, fertility and pregnancy covers that topic. For brand-specific pages, see ozempic.md.

The bottom line

GLP-1 drugs slow stomach emptying, and that can delay how other pills are absorbed. For birth control pills, measured evidence of a drop exists for tirzepatide only. The Mounjaro and Zepbound labels give a 4-week instruction, and the Foundayo label gives a 30-day instruction as a precaution. Semaglutide, liraglutide and dulaglutide labels give no contraception instruction, and their studies showed no meaningful change. For warfarin, levothyroxine and similar medicines, the labels call for monitoring.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Ozempic or Wegovy cancel out the birth control pill?

The semaglutide labels give no contraception instruction. A study of 43 women found that semaglutide 1.0 mg did not reduce exposure to ethinylestradiol or levonorgestrel. The labels still advise caution with oral medicines because semaglutide slows stomach emptying.

How long do the Mounjaro and Zepbound labels say to use backup contraception?

Both labels say to switch to a non-oral method or add a barrier method for 4 weeks after starting tirzepatide. The same 4 weeks apply after each dose escalation. The instruction covers oral hormonal contraceptives only.

Are IUDs, implants, patches, rings or shots affected by GLP-1 drugs?

The tirzepatide and orforglipron labels state that hormonal contraceptives that are not administered orally should not be affected. These methods do not pass through the stomach, so slow stomach emptying does not change their absorption.

Does the new weight-loss pill Foundayo affect birth control pills?

The Foundayo label says the effect of orforglipron on oral contraceptive absorption has not been evaluated in a clinical trial. As a precaution, it advises a non-oral method or an added barrier method for 30 days after starting and after each dose escalation.

Do GLP-1 drugs interact with thyroid medicine?

In a study with semaglutide tablets, levothyroxine exposure rose by 33%. The oral semaglutide and Wegovy labels say to consider increased clinical or laboratory monitoring for narrow therapeutic index drugs. Ask your prescriber whether your thyroid tests need a new schedule.
